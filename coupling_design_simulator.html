Interactive Mechanical Engineering Design and Analysis Tool

Aim

To design a rigid flange coupling and a flexible pin-bush coupling for a given power, speed and service condition, and to determine the shaft diameter, key size, hub and flange proportions and bolt (pin) diameter, and to check the resulting stresses and shaft misalignment.

Apparatus

  • Coupling Design Simulator (virtual, SI units)
  • Scaled side view and end view of the designed coupling
  • Observation table in the Results section

Procedure

  1. Read the Theory section to understand the design equations and assumptions.
  2. Open the Lab section and select the coupling type (rigid or flexible).
  3. Enter the transmitted power, shaft speed and service factor.
  4. Enter the allowable shaft shear stress, bolt shear stress and bearing stress, the number of bolts and the pitch-circle ratio.
  5. Enter the angular and parallel misalignment of the shafts.
  6. Observe the design torque, shaft diameter, key size and bolt diameter, and study the scaled drawings.
  7. Read the detailed calculation table and the safety analysis.
  8. Click Record reading to save the observation in the Results table.
  9. Change one parameter at a time (for example power, speed or number of bolts) and record at least 5 readings.
  10. Open the Results section to study the table, and note your observations in the Conclusion section.

Theory

A shaft coupling joins two shafts to transmit torque. A rigid flange coupling needs well-aligned shafts. A flexible pin-bush coupling tolerates small misalignment through its rubber bushes.

Torque

T = 9550 × P / N (T in N·m, P in kW, N in rpm)
Tdesign = T × Service factor

Shaft diameter (torsion)

d = ∛( 16 Tdesign / (π τ) )

The calculated diameter is rounded up to the next standard shaft size.

Key (parallel key)

Shear: L = 2T / (d · b · τkey), τkey = 0.75 τ (assumed)
Bearing: L = 4T / (d · h · σb)

The key section b × h is taken from the standard parallel-key table for the selected shaft diameter. The required key length is the larger of the two values and must not exceed the hub length.

Coupling proportions (assumed)

Hub outer diameter = 2d, Hub length = 1.5d, Flange thickness = 0.5d
Bolt pitch-circle diameter PCD = ratio × d

Bolts / pins (single shear)

T = n · (π/4) db² · τb · (PCD / 2)
db = √( 8T / (n π τb PCD) ) → next standard size

Bearing of bolt on flange

σ = 2T / (n · db · tf · PCD)

Assumptions

  • Static, uniform torque, with the service factor covering shock and overload
  • Load shared equally by all bolts (pins)
  • The flexible pin-bush coupling is treated with the same shear model as the rigid coupling (simplified)
  • Misalignment limits are illustrative screening values, not manufacturer limits
  • Preliminary design only; detailed hub, flange, fatigue and bush checks are required before manufacture

What to look for

  • Increase the power at constant speed: how does the design torque and shaft diameter change?
  • Increase the speed at constant power: what happens to the torque?
  • Change the number of bolts: how does the required bolt diameter change?
  • Compare the rigid and flexible coupling for the same misalignment.

Conclusion

  • The design torque follows T = 9550 P / N, so it rises with power and falls with speed.
  • The shaft diameter varies with the cube root of the design torque, then rounds up to a standard size.
  • A larger number of bolts, or a larger pitch circle, reduces the bolt diameter needed for the same torque.
  • A rigid flange coupling allows very little misalignment, while a flexible pin-bush coupling tolerates more.
  • The results are preliminary and valid only under the stated assumptions.
